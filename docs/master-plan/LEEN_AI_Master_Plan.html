1Purpose and how to use this document

This document holds everything needed to keep building LEEN AI without re-deciding things: the product idea, every decision made so far, how the core engine works, and what each future phase delivers.

  • Starting a new phase? Read chapter 8 for that phase, then the matching parts of chapter 6 (engine) and chapter 9 (tables and endpoints).
  • Wondering why something is the way it is? Chapter 3 (decisions) and chapter 12 (design calls).
  • Explaining the project to someone? Chapters 2, 5 and the two diagrams in chapters 5 and 6.
  • Plan changed? Edit the HTML source and regenerate this PDF (command in chapter 13). The repository copies of the roadmap (docs/ROADMAP.md) and engine design (docs/architecture/CANVAS_ENGINE.md) must stay in step with it.

The one-sentence version. LEEN AI reads a student's syllabus once, plans one hour of study every day, and teaches that hour on an AI-powered 2D canvas like a real tuition teacher: it draws and explains, asks questions, lets the student answer on the same board, checks their work in red pen, and adapts tomorrow's class to what it learned about them.

2Vision

Summarised from AI Personal Teacher SaaS: Comprehensive Product Plan. LEEN AI is meant to be a full-time teacher, not a doubt-solving tool: it teaches, guides, evaluates and improves a student continuously, and it understands each student's strengths and weaknesses.

Problems it solves

ProblemHow LEEN AI answers it
Lack of personalisation: every student gets the same contentA student model (mastery, pace, misconceptions, styles that work) shapes every explanation and every plan
Limited teacher availabilityThe canvas teacher is available any time, for classes and for doubts
Poor performance tracking: marks don't show understandingPer-topic mastery from every answer, not just test scores
Static content: books and videos don't adaptEvery explanation is generated live, at the student's level, and re-explained a different way when needed
Lack of discipline: no structured routineA daily one-hour class, planned automatically, with parents able to see attendance

Users

  • Students: primary users. School students, competitive-exam students (JEE, NEET) and self-learners.
  • Parents: monitor progress, receive reports and alerts.
  • Admin: manages the system.
  • Teachers (added by this project): create classes that students join with a code. More teacher features come in a future phase, after Phase 13.

5The daily tuition loop

This is the product. Everything in the roadmap exists to make this loop work and to report on it.

1. One-time setup

  • Onboarding (already built) asks for class, board, subjects, level and language. It gains: daily study minutes (default 60), study days, and an optional target exam and date (Boards, JEE, NEET).
  • Per subject, the syllabus or a book's contents page is uploaded once, as a photo or PDF, by the student or parent. The AI extracts Subject → Chapters → Topics. The student reviews it (rename, reorder, delete, add, mark "already done") and confirms. It stays editable.
  • Optionally, books and notes are uploaded too. They ground teaching through RAG, with page citations.

2. Today's class

  • One class a day on one subject. The Planner rotates subjects, favouring weak subjects, near exams, and subjects not studied for a while.
  • The plan shows the subject, the topic(s), an agenda with minutes per part, and why (new topic, due for revision, unfinished last time).
  • The student can switch today's subject or topic.
  • It is prepared when the student first opens Today (with a "preparing your class" state), or overnight when the stack is running.

3. The class: one hour on the canvas

PartDefaultWhat happens
Warm-up~5 min2–3 quick questions on revision-due topics of today's subject, including the last class's topic if it was this subject
Teach~25–30 minThe topic is taught in scenes (board pages). Each scene goes idea → visual → worked example, then a checkpoint question
Practice~20 minQuestions from easy to hard, answered by voice or text. The AI writes each answer on the board and marks it, gives hints on request, and shows the full solution on the canvas when an answer is wrong
Wrap-up~5 minA summary board, homework (3–5 questions), and a preview of the next class
  • The split adapts: a new topic is teach-heavy, a revision day is practice-heavy, a struggling student gets more examples.
  • The student can talk at any time, by voice or text. The AI never gets cut off: it finishes its current step, answers on the board, and carries on with what was left.
  • A failed checkpoint makes the AI re-explain a different way (another visual or analogy), at most twice; then it moves on and flags the topic as weak.
  • Pause and resume the same day: the class remembers where it was.

4. Check and adapt

Every answer is graded and updates the student model: mastery per topic, misconceptions, pace, and which explanation styles work. The next plan reads it.

5. Homework, tests, progress and parents

  • Homework: submitted as a photo of paper working or typed, graded in the background, returned with red-pen marks.
  • Tests: weekly and per chapter, timed and auto-graded; every mistake is explained on the canvas.
  • Progress: mastery, time spent, weak areas. Parents: daily attendance, minutes, topics, scores, alerts, class replays, and a weekly email.

Anti-goals. The design has failed, even if it technically works, if:

  • the canvas turns into a slideshow of static text;
  • the student can't speak up at any time, or the tutor loses its place when they do;
  • teaching drifts outside the student's uploaded syllabus;
  • grading says "wrong" without showing where.

6Core engine design

The core engine is the AI-powered 2D canvas and everything behind it. Figure 2 shows its layers.

Principles

  • The AI says what; the engine decides how it looks. The model emits semantic actions such as plot y = x^2 from -3 to 3 or triangle A(0,0) B(4,0) C(0,3). The engine computes geometry, layout and animation, so the model never computes pixels.
  • Plain data from the model. Every model output is validated against a pydantic schema before it reaches the browser or the database, as app/services/whiteboard/parser.py does today. A prompt-injected input can only change what gets drawn.
  • One AI agent per job. Every piece of work (reading the syllabus, planning the day, designing and teaching the lesson, answering doubts, setting and checking questions, homework, tests, coaching, progress insights, parent reports) belongs to its own agent, with its own prompt, output schema, model setting and token log. Agents call code tools for anything that can be computed (the math engine, the answer checker, the planner's scores), so they spend tokens only on judgment and words. Code is the plumbing: it routes work between agents, keeps time and stores results.
  • Short streamed steps. Each AI call covers one scene, one checkpoint or one grading. That fits the bridge's per-call limit, lets the class adapt between steps, and starts drawing within seconds.
  • Token-efficient by design. If something can be computed, the engine computes it with a math library; the AI only names it. See "Token efficiency" below.
  • Every session is an append-only event log. Replay, time spent, analytics, the parent view and continuous improvement (§7.14) all read it.

Layer 1 · Board Protocol v2 Built in Phase 4

The shared language between the AI and the canvas: one JSON action per line, streamed. The 15 v1 actions stay unchanged (caption, write, underline, box, erase, line, arrow, rect, circle, clear, pause, object, animate, stop, remove). Every visual also takes at: [x, y, w, h] and color; without at it stacks down the stage on the right (x 920–1540).

columnside: left | right | fullWhere writes without x/y flow; each column keeps its place
plotfns (≤4, functions of x), x [lo, hi], y?, points {A: [x, y]}, labels, shade {fn, from, to}, xlabel, ylabelCurves (mathjs, split at asymptotes), axes and ticks (d3-scale), shading

chartkind bar | pie | line, labels, values, title, unitScales, bars, slices, axes
flownodes, edges [[i, j]] (none = chain), kind chart | mind, dirLayout (dagre) or a radial mind map
diagramkind: circuit (parts, layout), lens / mirror (shape, f, u, h), fbd (forces), atom (z, symbol), cell (cell, labels), apparatus (items)The image by the lens or mirror formula, Bohr shells, symbol and label placement
Still to come: ask, mark and verdict (Phase 5, talking to the board); image and cite (Phase 6, photos and books).

Everything draws stroke by stroke, like the existing pencil. Adding a primitive means adding exactly three things: a pydantic model (backend), a renderer in the frontend registry (type → render(ctx, action, clock)), and a short section in the prompt's command reference.

Layer 2 · Talking to the board

The student never writes on the board; only the AI does. The student talks to it at any time, through a text box that is always open under the board, or a push-to-talk mic. The browser's speech recognition (en-IN) turns speech into text and sends it when they stop speaking; narration is turned down while the mic listens. Browsers without speech recognition show only the text box.

Student input never cuts the AI off. Like a real teacher, the AI finishes the step it is on, then works the message in:

  1. The AI writes a board turn in seconds, but playing it takes minutes, so the browser knows how far the board has played when a message arrives.
  2. The browser cuts at the end of the current step (the next caption not yet shown). Everything before it plays on.
  3. It sends the message with keep (how many of the turn's actions stay) straight away, so the AI works while the step finishes. If the AI is still writing the turn, the message waits until it is done.
  4. The server records the cut (cut_at) and gives the AI short summaries of the board so far and of the part not yet taught, plus the message.
  5. The AI acknowledges the message, answers it on the board, then teaches what was left, adapted to it: slower, skipped, or with another picture.

Questions and checking. ask (kind choice, number, expression or text) makes the board write the answer line and wait, and the talk bar becomes the answer box. The answer key never reaches the browser. Choice, number and expression answers are checked in code, instantly: a small safe evaluator (Python ast, no eval) reads fractions, roots and π, whole-number answers must be exact and decimals match to the places the student typed, and expressions match when they agree at several sample values. For number questions the AI sends the sum as calc, and code works out the key. The board writes the student's answer in orange with a tick or cross. A wrong answer gets one more try with the hint; after a second, the AI explains the mistake on the board and carries on. Text answers go to the AI, which copies the student's lines onto the board, marks each with mark (tick or cross) and records a verdict.

Before classes exist (Phase 5), this lives inside Ask a doubt: messages mid-explanation, checkpoint questions the AI chooses to ask, and "Now you try", a similar question after the explanation. The daily class reuses all of it.

Layer 3 · AI agents and the class conductor

Every job is done by an AI agent. Each agent is a prompt, an output schema and a model/effort setting; all of them call the model through the LLM gateway, and each call is logged under the agent's name.

AgentIts jobPhase
Doubt SolverAnswers doubts on the board, follow-ups, "explain another way", and the student's messages in the middle of an explanation3–5
ExaminerSets questions with answer keys (checkpoints, "Now you try", practice); checks answers, using the answer checker for choice, number and expression answers and judging written ones; explains mistakes on the board5
Syllabus ReaderReads a contents page or syllabus (photo or PDF) into chapters and topics6
LibrarianReads uploaded books and notes (scanned pages by vision), tags pages to syllabus topics, and finds the right pages for a topic or a doubt6
PlannerPlans each day: picks the subject and topic from scores computed in code, and writes the agenda and the "why"7
CoachKeeps the student model: mastery notes, misconceptions, pace and the explanation styles that work, after each class and answer7
Lesson DesignerDesigns the hour: scenes with goals, visuals and examples, checkpoints and practice; uses the student's books when uploaded8
Board TeacherTeaches each scene of the class on the board, reteaches a different way, and runs the wrap-up8
Homework AgentSets homework at the end of class, and checks submitted homework (photo or typed) in the background with red pen8–9
Test MakerBuilds weekly and chapter tests (mixed types, timed) from the topics studied9
Progress AnalystTurns the numbers (computed in code) into insights: weak areas, trends, what to study next10
Parent ReporterWrites the weekly parent report and the alert messages11

A small piece of code, the class conductor, routes work between the agents in this order and keeps the time budget (it trims extra practice when the class runs long):

planned → warmup → teach(scene i) ⇄ checkpoint → practice(question j) → wrapup → completed
            ↘ student message → reply → (carry on)
                                 checkpoint failed once or twice → reteach (a different way)
  • Planner scores. Code computes each subject's and topic's score: rotation weighted by weakness, exam date and days since last studied; syllabus order; revisions due (simplified SM-2 spaced repetition); unfinished topics; daily minutes. The Planner agent picks from the top candidates and explains its choice in a few lines.
  • Answer keys. For number questions the Examiner sends the sum (calc) and the answer checker computes the key; written questions carry a model answer. The student can say "I think that's wrong" and the Examiner re-checks.
  • Step streaming. Each POST /student/lessons/{id}/advance carries the student's input (an answer, a message, "next" or "hint") and streams the next step over SSE, ending at an ask or at the end of a segment. Same SSE events, 15-second keep-alive pings and save-on-disconnect as today's doubt stream.
  • Prefetch. While the student answers a checkpoint, the next scene is generated in the background.

Layer 4 · Student model (memory)

  • Topic mastery (per student and topic): score 0–1, confidence, attempts, correct answers, last practised, next review date, and a status (not started, learning, practising, mastered). Updated after every graded answer with a simple weighted (Elo-like) rule.
  • Learner profile: pace, explanation styles that worked, recent misconceptions, language mix. Maintained by the Coach.
  • Student card: every prompt gets a compact card (under 300 tokens) with class, board, level, language, mastery of the current topic and its prerequisites, top misconceptions, and styles that work.

Layer 5 · Syllabus and knowledge base (RAG)

  • Syllabus. A contents-page or syllabus photo or PDF goes to the Syllabus Reader; the student confirms the topic tree. It is the backbone for plans, mastery and tests.
  • Books and notes are ingested in the background (Celery): text extracted from PDFs, scanned pages read through the gateway's vision call, split into chunks by page and heading, embedded with a free local multilingual embedding model (no API key; works for Hindi), and stored in pgvector (Postgres image pgvector/pgvector:pg16).
  • Retrieval per topic feeds the Lesson Designer and Board Teacher; per doubt, the Doubt Solver. Boards cite pages with cite chips. Without books, the AI teaches from its own knowledge, kept to the syllabus topic and the student's board and class.
  • Synopsis alignment. This is the RAG pipeline the project synopsis promises; pgvector fills the vector-store role the synopsis names as Qdrant.

Layer 6 · LLM gateway

  • app/services/llm/ becomes the single way to call a model: stream_text(agent, system, prompt, images=[]) and complete_json(agent, system, prompt, schema, images=[]).
  • The provider is chosen by LLM_PROVIDER: claude_bridge (default) or anthropic_api (for deployment).
  • Model and effort are set per agent (Layer 3): board agents run fast (Sonnet, low effort); short writing jobs (Planner, Coach, Progress Analyst, Parent Reporter) use Haiku; reading photos and judging written answers use more effort.
  • Every call is logged in llm_calls: agent, duration, input, output and cached tokens, cost reported by the bridge, status.
  • Image input through the bridge is tested first thing in Phase 6 (the syllabus photo needs it), trying in order: (1) claude -p --input-format stream-json with image blocks; (2) the bridge saves the image in the run's empty folder and enables only Claude Code's Read tool for that run; (3) the Anthropic API for vision calls only, if Satyam agrees to an API key.

Token efficiency

AI tokens are the main running cost and the main source of delay, so the engine spends as few as possible. The rule: if it can be computed, the engine computes it; the AI only names it.

WhereHow tokens are saved
GraphsThe AI sends plot y=x^2, x from -3 to 3 (about 25 tokens). The engine evaluates the curve with a math library, instead of the AI writing out hundreds of points
Tables of valuesThe AI sends the expression and the x values; the engine fills in every cell
GeometryThe AI sends the vertices; the engine computes angles, arcs, lengths and label positions
DiagramsA template name and a few parameters (circuit: battery, R1=4Ω, bulb, in series) instead of 30+ separate line and arrow commands
FlowchartsNodes and edges only; a layout library places them
Layout and defaultsThe auto-layout cursor means most actions carry no coordinates; colour, size and position are omitted unless they differ from the default
GradingChoice, number and expression answers are checked in code by a small safe evaluator (exact whole numbers, decimals to the places typed, equivalence by sampling), at zero tokens. The AI judges only written answers
Answer keysFor number questions the AI sends the sum (calc) and code works out the key, instead of a second AI solve
Student messagesA reply in the middle of an explanation carries two short summaries (the board so far, the part still to teach), not the action lists. The request goes out while the current step is still playing, so the wait is hidden
Prompt sizeThe command reference is split by subject, so a physics doubt carries only the primitives it needs. Follow-ups get a short board summary (under 200 tokens), not the full action list. The student card stays under 300 tokens. Fixed prompt parts are cached (Claude Code caches automatically; the API provider marks them with cache_control)
Model routingEach agent gets the smallest model that does its job: Haiku for the Planner, Coach, Progress Analyst and Parent Reporter; Sonnet at low effort for the board agents; more effort only for reading photos and judging written answers
ReuseThe same doubt (same normalised question, class, board, level and language) is served from cache at zero tokens. Template diagrams cost nothing to redraw
MeasurementEvery call logs input, output and cached tokens in llm_calls, read from the bridge's result event. Phase 4 records a baseline from v1 doubts and must stay at or below it, despite the richer visuals

What changed from the old roadmap

  • The canvas is now the core engine that every teaching feature runs through.
  • Standalone "streaming chat" is dropped; follow-up questions happen on the board.
  • RAG stays early (Phase 6, on pgvector) and grounds the lessons.
  • Quizzes became homework and tests, answered by voice, text or photo.
  • Phase 5 redesign: the student talks to the board instead of writing on it.
  • Fully AI: every job has its own AI agent; teacher features move to a future phase.
  • The study planner became the daily tuition class.
  • Phase numbers changed: the old 8 phases are now 13.
  • The business model (§9) is out of scope.

8Phase-by-phase detail

Each phase lists its goal, the backend and frontend work, and the checks that mean it is done.

Phase 4Canvas engine v2: richer visuals Done

The AI can draw any common school visual cleanly, and a doubt becomes a multi-turn board session.

Result. On the 8 sample doubts the AI now uses about 60% fewer output tokens (median 593 vs 1,494.5) and half the commands (17.5 vs 33.5), while choosing plots, diagrams, tables, geometry and flowcharts on its own. Identical doubts are served from a cache with no AI call (68 ms in the live check).

Backend

  • LLM gateway in app/services/llm/: provider switch, per-agent model, effort and timeout, llm_calls log. The existing claude_bridge.stream_text becomes the bridge provider.
  • Protocol v2 schemas split out of whiteboard.py into a board schema package; ActionStreamParser reused.
  • Migrations 0004 (llm_calls) and 0005 (board_sessions, board_events, with existing doubts moved in; reversible). Doubt URLs stay the same.
  • Follow-ups and "explain another way": POST /student/doubts/{id}/turns (SSE) with a short board summary; 6 turns per board.
  • Identical doubts (same question and profile) are served from earlier boards with no AI call.
  • board-eval CLI: sample doubts, token report, fails if the median output grows.
  • Prompt command reference covers v2, with guidance on choosing a visual format, split by subject so each prompt carries only what it needs.
  • Token usage (input, output, cached) read from the bridge's result event into llm_calls; a baseline measured from v1 doubts.

Frontend

  • Engine extracted to features/canvas/.
  • Renderer registry, scenes with a thumbnail strip, camera focus, auto-layout cursor.
  • Renderers computed by the math engine: mathjs for plots and tables of values (never eval), d3-scale/d3-shape for axes and charts, dagre for flowcharts, in-house geometry helpers; plus diagram templates and the number line. (The image action moved to Phase 6, with photo doubts.)
  • Doubts page: follow-up box and "Explain another way" button.

Done when

  • A developer-only gallery renders every primitive.
  • Sample doubts in maths, physics, chemistry and biology each pick and draw a fitting visual.
  • Unit tests cover the expression parser, layout and schema validation.
  • Old doubts replay unchanged.
  • Median output tokens per doubt stay at or below the v1 baseline.

Phase 5Talk to the board Done

The student talks to the board by voice or text at any time. The AI finishes its step, works what they said into the explanation like a real teacher, asks questions and checks the answers.

Result. Live on the bridge, a question sent mid-explanation was answered after the current step ("Great question!"), on the same page, and the AI then taught what was left (867 output tokens, about 9 s). "Now you try" took 187 tokens; number answers were checked instantly at zero tokens; after two wrong answers the Examiner wrote the answer, crossed it and showed the working (288 tokens). Hindi students speak through Sarvam AI and hear Hindi narration.

Backend

  • Agents: the Doubt Solver (doubts, follow-ups, replies) and the Examiner (practice questions, answer checks), each with its own model setting and token log.
  • Board actions ask (choice, number, expression or text; the answer key is stripped before it reaches the browser), mark (a tick or cross on a written line) and verdict.
  • Mid-explanation replies: a turn carries the message and keep; the earlier turn records cut_at; the prompt carries summaries of the board so far and of the part still to teach.
  • "Check" turns (explain a wrong answer) and "Now you try" turns (a practice question); 12 turns per board.
  • POST /student/doubts/{id}/answers: choice, number and expression answers checked in code by a safe evaluator, with two tries; answers stored as board events.
  • A questions module in the board prompt.

Frontend

  • Talk bar under the board: a text box; a push-to-talk mic (browser speech recognition, en-IN, narration turned down while listening); answer boxes for each kind; hint; "Show me"; "Explain another way"; "Now you try".
  • Player: cut at the end of the current step, wait at an ask, write the student's answer with its tick or cross.
  • Replays show the student's messages and answers.

Done when

  • A message sent mid-explanation is answered after the current step, and the AI carries on with what was left instead of starting over.
  • Choice, number and expression answers are marked instantly with no AI call; a written answer is copied onto the board with the wrong line crossed in red.
  • "Now you try" sets a question and checks the answer.
  • Tests cover the evaluator and grading, the cut, the prompts and the player's question flow.

Phase 6Syllabus & knowledge base (RAG) Next

The AI knows each student's syllabus and can teach from their own books.

Backend

  • Agents: the Syllabus Reader and the Librarian.
  • Syllabus upload (photo or PDF) → Celery → Syllabus Reader (vision) → draft tree → student confirms.
  • syllabus_* tables, one tree per student and subject.
  • Books and notes: documents upload with size limits; Celery extracts PDF text, reads scanned pages by vision, splits into ~500-token chunks with page references, embeds with a small local multilingual model (chosen at build time), stores in pgvector with an HNSW index.
  • retrieve(student, query, subject/topic) searches only the student's own documents. Doubts use it, with cite.

Frontend

  • Syllabus page: upload, review the tree (edit, reorder, mark done), confirm.
  • Documents list with ingestion status.
  • cite chip that opens the page.

Done when

  • A contents-page photo becomes a correct tree.
  • A doubt cites the right textbook page.
  • Tests cover the chunker, and retrieval with fake embeddings.

Phase 7Student model & daily planner

The AI knows what the student has mastered and plans each day's class.

Backend

  • Onboarding fields: daily minutes, study days, exam and date.
  • topic_mastery updated from graded answers (now tagged with a topic); learner_profiles; student-card builder.
  • Agents: the Planner and the Coach.
  • Planner scores as pure, unit-tested functions (subject rotation, topic choice, spaced revision, agenda minutes); the Planner agent picks from the top candidates and writes the agenda and the "why".
  • daily_plans; GET /student/today (makes the plan if missing); POST /student/today/switch; overnight pre-generation by Celery beat at 02:00 IST.
  • question_items (moved from Phase 5), tagged with topics.
  • "Practice this topic": a short talk-to-the-board session that moves mastery, so the phase is useful before classes exist.

Frontend

  • New onboarding step.
  • Today page: agenda, why this topic, switch, practice.
  • Mastery chips on the Syllabus page.

Done when

  • On seeded data the scores rank the expected subject and topic first (unit tests), and the Planner agent's plan uses them (mocked gateway).
  • Practice moves mastery.
  • Today shows the plan.

Phase 8Daily tuition class (the core loop)

The full one-hour interactive class works end to end.

Backend

  • Agents: the Lesson Designer, the Board Teacher and the Homework Agent, with the Doubt Solver and Examiner from Phase 5.
  • Lesson Designer → lesson_outlines, made together with the plan.
  • Class orchestrator: state stored on the board session; steps warm-up, teach, checkpoint, reteach, practice, wrap-up, doubt; time budget; next scene prefetched and cached in Redis.
  • Endpoints: start or resume, advance (SSE), pause, replay.
  • Student messages work as in Phase 5: the AI finishes its step, answers, and carries on with the lesson.
  • Wrap-up: the Homework Agent sets homework (a row in assignments).
  • Coach summary: mastery and profile updates, plus the summary parents will see.

Frontend

  • Class player at /student/lesson/:id: agenda progress, timer, talk bar, answer boxes, scene strip, end-of-class summary.

Done when

  • The demo script (chapter 11) passes end to end on the live bridge.
  • The state machine is unit-tested with fake agents.
  • A class resumes after a page reload.

Frontend

11Demo script

The definition of done for the core loop, at the end of Phase 8. It is also the script for showing the project to evaluators.

  1. The student uploads a photo of their physics book's contents page once, and confirms the topic tree. They upload the textbook PDF too.
  2. Today's class shows a 60-minute Physics plan for "Motion in a straight line", chosen by subject rotation.
  3. In class, the AI draws a velocity–time graph and cites the textbook page.
  4. It asks a checkpoint question. The student says the working aloud; the AI writes it on the board, crosses line 2 in red, and re-explains with a different picture.
  5. Mid-scene the student types a doubt. The AI finishes its sentence, answers it on the board, and carries on with the lesson.
  6. Practice answers are typed or spoken and marked on the board. Homework is set. The mastery bar for that topic moves.

Later phases extend the demo: the homework photo comes back marked (Phase 9), the progress page shows the mastery change (Phase 10), and the parent sees today's class and the weekly report (Phase 11).

12Design calls and open items

Design calls

Made while planning; each can be overridden.

  • The engine stays on the Claude Code bridge behind a provider switch. The image fallback enables only the Read tool; a paid API key is used only with Satyam's agreement.
  • A free local embedding model feeds pgvector, so no API key is needed.
  • Math engine libraries: mathjs, d3-scale/d3-shape and dagre in the browser; a small safe evaluator for answer checking on the backend.
  • Today's plan is made on the first open of the day, and overnight too when the stack is running.
  • Daily minutes default to 60. Minutes and study days are per student, not per subject.
  • An AI agent for every job; code only routes work between them and does the maths they call on.
  • Class split: warm-up 5, teach 25–30, practice 20, wrap-up 5 minutes, adapting per day. At most two reteaches per checkpoint.
  • Two ways to talk to the board: voice and text, never writing on it. A spoken message is sent when the student stops speaking.
  • Two tries for a code-checked answer; after that the AI explains the mistake on the board.
  • Phase 5 lives inside Ask a doubt, so it ships something usable before classes exist.
  • Teachers keep what Phase 2 built (classes with join codes); more teacher features come in a future phase, after Phase 13 (Satyam, 18 Oct 2026).
  • Standalone chat is dropped in favour of follow-up questions on the canvas.
  • The engine design lives in docs/architecture/CANVAS_ENGINE.md, next to ARCHITECTURE.md.

Open items

  • Result of the image-input spike (start of Phase 6).
  • Which local embedding model (Phase 6): small, multilingual, 384-dimensional.
  • Chart library for analytics (Phase 10).
  • Licensed TTS vendor for production (Phase 13).

13Working agreement

  • One phase per session. Branch feature/phase-N-<slug> from main, or stacked on the previous phase while that one is unmerged.
  • Each phase delivers backend and frontend together, with tests: pytest with a mocked LLM gateway; vitest for engine maths and layout.
  • Follow the existing patterns: feature folders on the frontend; endpoints → services → repositories on the backend; the response envelope; require_roles; reversible migrations.
  • When a phase ends:
    • update the status in docs/ROADMAP.md;
    • update CANVAS_ENGINE.md and ARCHITECTURE.md if the design moved;
    • update the "Soon" labels in frontend/src/config/navigation.js and the dashboards;
    • regenerate this PDF if the plan changed.
